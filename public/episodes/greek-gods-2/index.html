Greek Gods · Part 2

Marriage, Harvest or Hearth: Which Goddess Held the Real Power?

Hera, Demeter and Hestia got no kingdom of their own. Each found a different kind of power.

About this episode

Three brothers drew lots for the universe. Their three sisters were never given one, and yet one of them stopped the grain for a whole year until the king of the sky gave in.

Hera, Demeter and Hestia were swallowed by their father Cronus and brought back up by Zeus, just like their brothers. But when Zeus, Poseidon and Hades divided the sky, the sea and the dead, the sisters got no kingdom. In Greek mythology, they got something smaller and closer to home: a marriage, a harvest, and a fire in the middle of the house.

Hera became queen of the gods, married to the least faithful husband in Greek myth, and still her temple at Olympia stood for more than a century before his. Demeter, whose daughter Persephone was carried down to the land of the dead, found a way to fight back without a kingdom of her own. And Hestia, asked in marriage by Poseidon and Apollo, swore never to marry at all.

This is Part 2 of our Greek Gods series. Next: three gods who were never supposed to be born, one of them straight out of her father's head.
