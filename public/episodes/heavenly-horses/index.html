Han China · 104–102 BC

A Kingdom Refused to Sell Its Horses. So China Sent an Army

Dayuan would not sell its horses. The emperor would not let his beaten army back through the Jade Gate.

About this episode

Emperor Wu of Han offered a thousand pieces of gold and a horse made of gold for the best horses of a kingdom far to the west. Its nobles refused. They had done the arithmetic, and they were sure China could never send an army that far.

In the second century BCE, the most dangerous enemy of the Han dynasty was the Xiongnu, nomads of the northern steppe who lived on horseback, and to catch them the emperor needed better horses than his own. More than two thousand miles away, in the Ferghana Valley, the kingdom of Dayuan had horses that, people said, sweated blood and were descended from horses of heaven. The envoy sent to buy them lost his temper, smashed the golden horse, and was ambushed and killed at Dayuan's border. So began the War of the Heavenly Horses.

When the first army limped back to the frontier with one or two men in ten, the emperor shut the Jade Gate against his own soldiers. Then he sent sixty thousand more, with a hundred thousand oxen and two experts in judging horses, and at a capital with no wells, the Han moved the river. The court historian Sima Qian was blunt about what it all cost, and about who was to blame.
