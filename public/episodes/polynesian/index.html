The Pacific

How Humans Learned to Cross Entire Oceans Without a Compass

Polynesian navigators found islands by reading the stars, the swells, the birds and the clouds.

About this episode

How do you cross thousands of kilometers of open ocean when you have no compass and no land in sight? For centuries, Polynesian wayfinders found their way across the Pacific by learning to read signals hidden in the natural world.

Stars, winds, ocean swells, clouds, currents and even birds could reveal what was happening beyond the horizon. This knowledge was memorized and passed from navigator to navigator, allowing skilled wayfinders to navigate enormous stretches of ocean without modern instruments.

In 1976, the voyaging canoe Hōkūleʻa sailed from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti using traditional non-instrument navigation, with Mau Piailug serving as navigator. The voyage helped demonstrate the remarkable knowledge behind traditional Pacific wayfinding.
