Greece · 490 BC

The Battle of Marathon: The Battle Won by a Dead Man's Army

Athens against the Persian landing at Marathon, told from an unexpected angle.

About this episode

What if the warriors who died on a battlefield were said to keep fighting long after the battle was over?

In 490 BC, a Persian army landed at Marathon with Athens as its target. Outnumbered and divided over whether to attack, the Athenians ultimately chose to fight — and then did something the Persians never expected: they ran toward them.

The battle became a brutal clash of shields, spears and close combat before the Athenian wings turned inward and trapped the Persian center. Even after victory, the Athenians had to race back to Athens to stop the Persian fleet from reaching the city. But the strangest part of the story came centuries later.

According to ancient traditions, Marathon was not truly silent after dark. People claimed they could still hear horses and men fighting on the battlefield — as if the warriors who died there had never stopped fighting. Other stories told of mysterious warriors and unexplained encounters surrounding the battle.

We cannot know whether the legends were true. But Marathon became more than a battlefield: a place where, according to legend, the living won the battle... and the dead never stopped fighting.
